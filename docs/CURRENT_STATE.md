# Kainos Player current state

What the app does today, what it does not do, and how each claim was checked. Written 2026-11-11 for `main` at `9c1fad7`. Dated history lives in git; `docs/reviews/` holds the review reports behind the latest merges.

## What works

**First run.** Home asks where the listener's music lives and shows each source's real state, with one action that goes to the fix. Local files lead because they need no account. The screen leaves once music exists. Nothing blocks the rest of the app. There is no sample content.

**Local files.** Choose folders on either platform. Linux scans `~/Music` until folders are chosen, and an explicitly empty list stays empty. Android uses persisted SAF folder access, with MediaStore as an optional extra source that the first chosen folder turns off. Both platforms load a saved scan at once and rescan in the background. Linux plays through headless mpv, which must be installed (it runs with `--no-config` and shows as "Kainos Player" in the audio mixer). Android plays through Media3 in a foreground service. Files carry album artist, track and disc number, year and genre: desktop reads them with ffprobe (tag keys matched case-insensitively), and Android reads them lazily with `MediaMetadataRetriever` on first play and in a pass after each scan. Files without tags get a title and artist from their file name after one shared cleaner removes download prefixes, video ids and bitrate suffixes. Albums group by album artist and play in disc, track and title order. If a tag pass changes an album's id, Home pins to it stay valid.

**Spotify.** Search, liked songs and playlists use the Web API with the listener's own Client ID. Playback uses librespot as a Connect receiver: a helper process on Linux, in-process on Android. It needs Spotify Premium and a separate one-time sign-in. Spotify Loud normalization runs in the receiver at +3 dB. Both receivers cache the audio they fetch (Linux `--cache-size-limit 2G`; Android 1 GiB, cleared when found above that) so a replayed song starts from disk, and both turn librespot's autoplay off. Android streams a song while it downloads instead of waiting for the whole file, and rebuilds its receiver after a network change. On Android the receiver also queues the next Spotify song while one plays, so librespot opens it about 20 seconds before the end and moves into it without a new load; a receiver whose connection to Spotify is down is rebuilt before a load, and an idle one as soon as a validated network is up (added 2026-11-14, covered by unit tests, not yet tried on the phone). Android radio and Discover Weekly read through the playback receiver's session and retry after 1, 3 and 8 seconds, and the last Discover Weekly loaded for each account is kept so the Home shelf survives a failed refresh. On Linux, `scripts/install-librespot.sh` builds the `kainos-discover-weekly` helper (Discover Weekly, playlists, radio) and refuses to install one without radio mode. A start waits once for a Retry-After of 5 seconds or less. A longer rate limit pauses playback with a message and does not skip the track. A rejected token is refreshed once, and a start goes directly to the known Kainos device. Liked Songs and the listener's playlists are saved in `kainos.db` per Spotify account and shown at launch before any network call. A refresh pages Liked Songs from the newest until it reaches a saved song and re-reads only playlists whose snapshot changed, one at a time in the background. While Spotify is offline or rate limited, Library keeps the saved copy and says how old it is. Signing out or switching account deletes it.

**YouTube.** Search uses the listener's own YouTube Data API key. Audio comes from yt-dlp and mpv on Linux, and NewPipe Extractor and ExoPlayer on Android. Hearted tracks can cache audio for offline play.

**Search.** Spotify and YouTube only. Library has its own fuzzy search for everything else. Starting a Search result plays that track and then its own source's radio: Spotify radio through librespot, or the video's YouTube Mix. Radio never mixes sources and never falls back to a name search or a Liked Songs shuffle. Settings → Playback can turn it off. On Linux, Spotify radio needs the optional `kainos-discover-weekly` helper. Spotify and YouTube playlists in the results open as an in-app page: their songs load 50 at a time as the list scrolls, Play and Shuffle load the rest and queue the whole playlist, each row has the song menu, and the page's ⋮ menu still opens the playlist on Spotify or YouTube. Back or Escape returns to the results where they were. Spotify playlists the Web API refuses load through librespot instead.

**Library and playlists.** Songs, albums, artists and playlists, with Local files only and Favorites only filters, and sorts by name, duration, artist, album, year and recently added. Shuffle sits beside Play all above the list. App hearts stay separate from Spotify Liked Songs. Every song row in Library, Search and Home's Continue listening and history shelves has a menu (the ⋮ button, a long press or a right click) with Play next, Add to queue, Heart, Add to playlist, Go to album, Go to artist and Start radio; Search rows that cannot play in the app skip the queue actions, and local files have no radio. Hearts can also be set from Now Playing.

**Collection pages.** Albums, artists, and Kainos, Spotify and YouTube playlists open as pages with Play, Shuffle and the song menu. Each main tab keeps its own stack of pages, and Back or Escape closes the top page and returns with its scroll kept. Search lists Spotify Albums and Artists beside the songs. An artist endpoint that Spotify refuses with 403 shows that, and Kainos does not guess from names. A Kainos playlist page removes entries with undo and reorders them by drag or from the row menu. Entries whose file is missing on this device are marked.

**Hide duplicates.** Settings → Library → Hide duplicates (on by default) lists a recording once when it comes from more than one source. Two copies match on title and primary artist (case, accents and punctuation folded) and, when both are known, durations within 3 seconds. A suffix such as "Live" or "Remix" keeps a version distinct, remaster and "official video" notes do not. The local file is kept first, then Spotify, then YouTube. It changes only what lists show and queue, not what is stored. A heart on a hidden copy shows on the listed one.

**Queue.** Shuffle, repeat, reordering, removal with undo, saving as a Kainos playlist, the song menu on every row, dense rows, and Clear upcoming (undoable; it keeps the played and playing songs). While a source's radio feeds the queue, a footer says so and Stop radio ends it; the queued songs stay.

**Listening history.** Each device keeps its own play history in `kainos.db`: one row per listen with the track, start time, time heard and whether it finished. History is never synced and never leaves the device, and events older than a year are pruned. Home shows up to three shelves from it, each hidden when empty: On repeat (at least two finished or 30-second plays in the last 30 days), Rediscover (hearted songs last played over 60 days ago) and Recently added (local files by modification time). Tapping a row plays that shelf from there. A listen still in progress when the app quits is not recorded.

**Now Playing.** Seek, volume, heart, sleep timer, source and quality details, the volume-levelling gain applied, an album line with links to the album and artist pages, retry and another-source fallback, and changing a Spotify track's YouTube cache match.

**Gapless.** Local files play gaplessly on both platforms, including across a seek. On Android the next local file waits in ExoPlayer behind the playing one, and the session swaps it when a skip, Play next, shuffle or repeat change picks another. A hearted YouTube track whose audio is cached plays as a local file, so it joins gaplessly too. Streamed Spotify and YouTube tracks have a short gap. A skip after a failed track keeps the paused player on Android instead of going idle.

**Offline.** Availability is separate from hearts: local, cached, downloading, waiting, unavailable or failed. Settings → Downloads shows progress, retry, removal, pinning and storage use, against a 2 GiB budget. Spotify audio is never saved as a playable download; the receivers' own disk cache only speeds up a replay. A Spotify heart can cache a matched YouTube recording, labeled as a match. One video that cannot be downloaded fails with the extractor's own reason and never holds up the rest; a network or server error retries at the back of the queue (30 s, 2 min, 8 min, then Failed). "Waiting for network" appears only while offline. On Android, "Download on Wi-Fi only" (on by default) holds downloads on metered networks as "Waiting for Wi-Fi". Remove (or Clear hearted audio cache) keeps the heart but leaves the audio off, across restarts, until Download is tapped. When failures look like an outdated extractor, Downloads says so once with the fix: `scripts/install-yt-dlp.sh` on Linux (it updates every copy), an app update on Android. Desktop cached files carry the real codec and bitrate.

**Session.** The queue, shuffle, repeat, position and the radio feeding the queue restore paused after a restart. Stream URLs are never stored.

**System controls.** MPRIS and media keys on Linux. Notification, lock screen and Bluetooth transport on Android, where tapping the notification opens Now Playing.

**Home sync.** The phone finds the PC on the local network. Both screens show a code to compare and confirm, and pairing pins the hub's certificate over HTTPS on port `43822`. YouTube and local-file hearts merge both ways. Spotify app hearts merge too, but only between devices signed into the same Spotify account, and never touch Spotify Liked Songs. Kainos playlists sync, and vault audio moves only after confirmation. Downloaded offline copies of hearted YouTube tracks are offered the same way when one device has the file and the other lacks it; a Spotify heart's YouTube match crosses only between devices on the same Spotify account. A copy is checked by size and SHA-256 before it enters the receiver's cache, respects that cache's budget and pins, and plays offline there as Cached. An interrupted copy leaves nothing behind and starts over on the next Transfer. Home pins for Kainos playlists and Spotify playlists sync with their order and unpins: the newest pin or unpin of a playlist wins, the most recent reorder decides the order, and Spotify pins cross only between devices on the same Spotify account. A pin for a Kainos playlist the other device does not have yet appears there once that playlist arrives. Album and folder pins stay on their device. A hub from before pin sync leaves pins alone. Paired devices can see and control each other from Now Playing, and Play here moves the other device's queue to this one. The PC also sends its loudness measurements of local files, which the phone uses for levelling (see below); a hub from before a route existed answers 404 and the phone skips it. Sync runs while the phone app is open. Opening the app syncs only when it has not synced yet in this run, the last sync was at least 10 minutes ago, or hearts, playlists or pins changed on the phone; Sync now always runs. A skipped copy stays skipped until its file changes or the listener taps Un-skip in Settings → Home sync → Skipped. Pending copies, skipped copies and conflicts have a checkbox per row and Select all: Transfer, Skip, Un-skip, Keep local and Keep remote act on the ticked rows, or on every row when none is ticked (added 2026-11-12, covered by Compose UI and service tests, not yet tried with a real pairing). The PC hub can start at login as a systemd user unit and then runs until it is stopped; it has no idle timeout, and it exits 0 only when another process already owns the hub. A hub-only process binds no media keys, restores no queue and starts no downloads.

**Backup.** Settings → Backup exports one JSON file (`kainos-backup` version 1) through the platform save picker: hearts, Kainos playlists, Home pins for playlists, and appearance, playback and Library preferences. It never holds sign-ins, keys, pairing, stream URLs, folder access or paths; local songs travel as content keys and file names. Restoring shows what the file would add, then adds it and removes nothing: missing hearts come back, the newer copy of a playlist wins, a playlist deleted here comes back, and Spotify hearts and pins apply only on the same Spotify account. Restoring the same file again changes nothing. Audio files are not included.

**Appearance.** Folio, Caelestia and Frost skins, seven tints, and light, dark or system mode. Caelestia follows the shell's live colours on Linux. Desktop has a collapsible, resizable Now Playing pane that waits for the first track, a compact player, and a dock: drag the pane's grip to any window edge (a tall portrait window docks at the bottom). Queue sits beside the content on windows 1200 dp wide and up. From 2000 dp it becomes a 400 dp frame column, Library Songs gets its own pane, Search shows playlists beside the track table, and Home and Settings spread into columns (Home from 1900 dp, Settings from 1700 dp).

**Echo.** `tools/echo-gateway` is a separate service that plays shuffled local folders on an Echo. It has its own queue and does not touch the player. It runs from the main checkout's `tools/echo-gateway` (the systemd user unit `kainos-echo-gateway.service` points there), and its loopback-only now-playing API listens on `127.0.0.1:8788`.

## What does not work

- Volume levelling (Settings → Playback, on by default) uses a local file's ReplayGain tags, or else its measured EBU R128 loudness. The desktop measures new and changed library files with ffmpeg after each scan (about 0.4 s per track at nice 19; roughly 4 minutes for the 561-file library the first time) and hearted YouTube downloads when they finish. Existing cached files join the next background pass; unchanged files are not measured again. Analysis is serialized, uses idle I/O priority when available, and is cancelled on rescan or JVM exit. Phones cannot measure: they use the PC's values received over Home sync (`/kainos-sync/v3/loudness`, matched by content key, then unique basename), so a phone file the PC never measured stays unlevelled. The target is Spotify Loud (-11 LUFS); a boost stops 1 dB under the measured true peak, and a file already peaking above that plays at unity rather than being cut. A complete tagged album uses its aggregate gain from the first played track, including a mid-album start, so gapless joins keep one gain. Both platforms change the gain on the first sample of the next file. Linux skips Opus and Matroska tags. YouTube streams (not cached) play at their own level. Spotify's normalization is built into the receiver and has no switch. Android levelling of analysed files is not yet verified on the phone.
- No gapless for Spotify or YouTube. No crossfade, EQ or DSP.
- No lyrics. They are dropped, not deferred; [the investigation](plans/lyrics-source-investigation.md) stays as the closed record.
- Kainos never writes to Spotify Liked Songs. The branch that would have added a choice to do so was dropped.
- YouTube is a fallback source for songs Spotify lacks. It is repaired when it breaks and not extended: no YouTube Music account library, no new YouTube features.
- No YouTube Music account library.
- Cached audio copies only while Sync now's Transfer runs in the open app. An interrupted copy restarts from the beginning.
- No sync while the phone app is closed. Pairing can start from the QR code the PC shows; the phone still compares the 6-digit code.
- Spotify Discover Weekly cannot be listed through the Web API for most developer apps. Paste its share link in Settings → Music sources.
- Spotify playback rests on unofficial librespot, so a protocol change can break it. Web API quotas can block search and library loading even while audio works.

More detail is in [provider limitations](LIMITATIONS.md).

## How this was verified

**Automated, on every change.** `./gradlew jvmTest` runs the unit tests of every module and the Compose UI tests in `shared/src/jvmTest`, which drive real screens with clicks and mouse-wheel input without a display. CI also runs `:core:data:verifyCommonMainKainosDatabaseMigration`, `:desktopApp:createDistributable`, `:androidApp:assembleDebug`, `:androidApp:assembleOptimized`, `:androidApp:lintDebug` and the gapless stress test against real mpv. Other tests that need real tools or a harness report as skipped, not passed. There is no emulator job.

**Automated, with a device attached.** `./gradlew :androidApp:connectedDebugAndroidTest` covers local scanning and playback, controls, completion, missing-file recovery, background service behaviour and simulated Spotify-to-local transitions.

**Release gate.** `scripts/android-release-gate.py --serial <emulator>` builds the optimized APK, verifies its signature and contents, smoke-tests that exact APK, drives it black-box through the phone paths (media keys as a headset or lock screen sends them, the media notification, ten rapid skips, 50 s of playback with the screen off across a natural track end, a background foreground-service start with every media exemption stripped, the notification opening Now Playing, restart restoring paused, and the track menu's Play next), runs the instrumented classes and the first-run journey against an R8 build, and prints the manual checks it cannot do as an unticked list. `--phone-paths <apk>` runs only the phone paths, in about two minutes. Releases still publish the debug APK: the optimized build becomes the default only after the owner has run that list on the phone. `androidApp/README.md` covers versions, rollback, signing and adopting a release key.

**Review of the latest merges.** Each branch merged on 2026-11-11 was reviewed and fixed on its own head with the full `jvmTest`, Android host test, migration verification, Android debug assembly and desktop distribution run (see `docs/reviews/`: collection pages, 1,693 of 1,708 cases passed with 15 skipped; loudness, 1,460 of 1,476 plus 265 Android host cases, with 16 skipped). Collection pages were also rendered headless on a private X display in light Frost and light Caelestia. Those runs predate the final merges, and the combined head `9c1fad7` has no recorded full run: run the commands above before relying on a count.

**By hand on real hardware, before this program.** On a Poco F7 Ultra running HyperOS: local playback, embedded artwork, notification actions, cold launch from the notification, and media island behaviour. On Linux: librespot sign-in, headless restart and audio start. Legacy JSON data imported into the database with matching counts.

**Not verified on real hardware: this whole program.** The phone has not run anything merged on 2026-11-11: streamed Spotify audio and its 1 GiB cache, the network-change receiver rebuild and the faster recovery after a takeover, Android radio and Discover Weekly through the receiver session, the rate-limit pause, Android tag reading and sorts, albums, artists and collection pages, Hide duplicates, the queue changes, Android volume levelling from the PC's measurements, Download on Wi-Fi only and the download failure handling, the kept paused Exo item after a failed skip, and the version code that now carries the schema. On the desktop the owner has not tried the ultrawide layouts, the dock motion or the Frost size fixes live. The desktop's loudness pass, hub-only changes and Spotify receiver cache are covered by tests and by review, not by long use.

**Also not verified on real hardware.** Bluetooth transport, live Spotify and YouTube notification behaviour, lock screen controls, rapid skips on HyperOS, Spotify background survival on Android, the 2026-11-02 Spotify reconnect and foreground-denial fixes, the track menu, Ready offline, sleep fade, listening history and volume levelling on Android, and old-to-new phone and PC sync. The release gate proves the stock-Android half of the transport, lock screen, rapid skip and background paths on the emulator, and lists the rest as manual checks. On the emulator Android logs the stripped background start as denied by policy yet admits it, so the app's denial handler still has not run on a device.

Run the checks with the commands under **Development and diagnostics** in the [README](../README.md).
