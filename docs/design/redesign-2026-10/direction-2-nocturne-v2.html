Direction 2, revisedNocturne v2

Same room, different walls. v1 was a blue-black night with a cold blue accent, which is the dark music app everyone ships. v2 keeps what you liked, the cover blurred and glowing behind the player and its sampled colour re-tinting the app for every song, and changes two things: the base is now one of three warmer, more specific nights, and the seek bar is no longer a slider but the filament that lights the room. Use the switcher at the top right to flip every screen between the three palettes and v1.

Darkroom recommended

Oxblood and plum, the red safelight of a photographic darkroom. Warm enough that every cover looks lamp-lit, dark enough that a blue cover still reads as moonlight through it. Secondary text is a dusty rose, not grey. With no cover, the accent is lamp amber.

Clamp: sample to HSL, saturation 0.5–0.7, lightness 0.62–0.72. A sample within 25° of the base hue (reds, pinks) is lifted to lightness 0.74 and saturation 0.5 so it does not sink into the wall. Covers with saturation under 0.2 use the lamp.

Pine

Deep green-teal, a forest after midnight. The coolest of the three without being blue. It flatters warm covers, which glow like a fire against it, and gives Spotify's green source dot a home. With no cover, the accent is celadon.

Clamp: same ranges. Greens within 25° of the base are lifted to 0.74. Red samples are turned 12° toward orange and lifted, so red-on-green reads as a lamp rather than a holiday.

Smoke

Violet-graphite, stage haze under a coloured light. Close to neutral, so it accepts any sampled colour without a fight; the quietest of the three and the safest if most of your art is Spotify covers you did not choose. With no cover, the accent is heather.

Clamp: same ranges. Violets within 25° of the base are lifted to 0.74. Nothing else needs special handling.

What stays from v1

One family, Neue Haas Unica Light and Regular (Helvetica Neue / Segoe UI fallbacks here). Three layers from the same cover: the blurred cover at 1.5× behind the Now Playing surface, a vertical vignette in the palette's night colour, and the clamped sample as the live accent. In Compose: Modifier.blur on the cover painter and rememberAlbumLight promoted to primary; each palette is one ColorScheme plus the clamp parameters, with Dawn kept as the light scheme.

Seek bar

v1 used a 4 px bar with a flat fill, which is the slider every player ships. Four alternatives are drawn at phone size below. Filament is applied to the phone, the desktop pane and the mini player here: the played part is a lit wire in the song's own colour, ending in a small white bead; touching it thickens the wire, grows the bead and shows the time. It needs nothing from the source, so Spotify and YouTube get the same bar as a local FLAC, and on a Canvas it is two lines and a circle. The track is drawn across the full width so the length of the song stays legible.

A. Filament recommended

The played part is a hot wire: a 2 px line that brightens from the cover colour to white at the playhead, with a soft bloom around it; the unplayed part is the same wire unlit. While scrubbing the wire thickens to 3 px, the bead grows to 16 px with a halo and a time bubble sits above it. It is the only element on Now Playing that is brighter than the cover, so the eye lands on it.

In Compose: a Canvas with drawLine for the dark track, three drawLine strokes of increasing width and falling alpha for the bloom (no blur filter needed), a Brush.horizontalGradient for the wire, and drawCircle for the bead. Drag state animates two floats. Cheapest of the four after Swell.

B. Swell

At rest a 3 px hairline with a gradient from the cover's deep tone to its light tone, no handle. Under a finger it swells to a 14 px rounded bar and a 20 px white handle appears, cut out of the bar with a ring of the night colour. The change in weight is the feedback: nothing else on the screen moves.

In Compose: one drawRoundRect for the track, one with a horizontal brush for the fill, one circle for the handle, and an animateFloatAsState on the height. The cheapest to build; closest to what iOS does, so the least specific to Nocturne.

C. Rule

A ruler: a baseline with a tick every 15 seconds and a taller one every minute, so a 5:48 song and a 48-minute live set look different before you touch them. The elapsed time rides on the playhead instead of sitting at the left, and grows while scrubbing; the total stays at the right. The played part is a 3 px fill in the cover colour over the baseline.

In Compose: a loop of drawLine ticks (count from duration, so it is cheap to lay out), one fill line, one playhead line, and a Text offset by the played fraction, clamped to the edges. Works for every source; the tick density needs a rule for very long YouTube videos (switch to 1 min / 5 min ticks past 20 minutes).

D. Loudness

Sixty-four bars of the track's loudness, mirrored around the centre line; the played part is lit in the cover colour with a soft glow, the rest is dim. You can see the quiet intro and the chorus before you get there, and scrub to them. Scrubbing stretches the bars and shows the time bubble.

In Compose: drawing is easy (one drawRect per bar, played bars in the accent), but the data is not. Local files need a decode pass per track to measure loudness (ffmpeg astats on desktop, MediaCodec on Android), stored in kainos.db next to the scan snapshot. Spotify via librespot and YouTube streams expose no loudness profile, so they would fall back to Filament, which means two different bars in one app. Best kept as an optional extra for local files, not the default.
